# Principles

These apply to every holo drawn by this skill, including what is not yet named. When a principle guides a choice, cite it by name.

## Result

The principles in this list say the qualities the result will have. A Mandalorian who orders an armour asks for this: that it stops a blaster shot and weighs little. That is why they want it in beskar.

**UNDERSTOOD** — The only measure of a holo is that a human understands the thing sooner and better than without it. Pretty, aligned, readable by a machine count only when they serve that.

**AT_A_GLANCE** — A holo is looked at, not read: cover every word and the drawing still says its thing. If what is left is a grid of boxes, it is a table, and a table is not a holo.

**PADAWAN** — A holo is drawn for whoever meets the thing for the first time, not for whoever already knows it. How much to say, and in what form, is decided by what it explains.

**ONE_HOLO** — One holo says one thing. The second idea is a second holo.

**EVERYTHING_MEANS** — Every mark on the page means something: position first, then colour, stroke width, dashes, shape, empty space. Where a thing stands says whose it is or where it belongs; colour confirms, it does not replace. Nothing is ornament; what means nothing is not there.

**HUMAN_LINE** — Hand-drawn strokes and hand-written letters: it looks made for you, in a minute, by a person.

**NEVER_FINAL** — A holo is not a closed delivery: the source stays beside it, so whoever reads it changes it, by hand or with Claude.

**READER_DECIDES** — A holo is right when whoever reads it says so, not whoever drew it: it arrives ready to be redone.

## Work

The principles in this list say how we work to get there. The Armorer works one piece at a time, and nothing leaves the forge that she has not hammered herself: it holds for a helmet and it holds for a ring. This is the forge.
